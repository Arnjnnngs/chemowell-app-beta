ChemoWell — where it is, and where it should be

Left is the app as it ships today. Right is the proposed direction. The reference point is the category leader: Medisafe's home screen is a timeline of what is due when, and ChemoWell's is a stack of data-entry forms. That is the structural gap; the colour and the type are downstream of it.

Proposed

What changed and why

  • It answers the question first. Today the screen opens on three boxes asking for numbers. The proposal opens with the next dose, the time, and one button — and a ring showing 4 of 7 doses done.
  • A real timeline. Time on the left rail, taken doses checked and dimmed, the next one lifted. This is the pattern the category leader uses and it is the right one.
  • Vitals become readings, not forms. Three compact tiles showing the last value and when. Tap to log.
  • Colour finally means something. Today the orange "Log" buttons and a genuine overdose warning are the same colour, so a warning has no colour left to be. Terracotta = action, green = done, red = a limit. Only one thing on the screen is red.
  • The header drops from about 320px to 56px. The name, the date and the BETA strip were taking a fifth of the phone before any content.
  • The gradient is demoted. It survives inside the hero card, where it is a brand cue, instead of sitting behind every white card and flattening the hierarchy.

Proposed — empty

What changed and why

  • A real defect, not a style problem. With no medications added, today's Meds screen is roughly a thousand pixels of blank gradient. No message, no illustration, nothing to tap. It is the first screen a brand-new user reaches.
  • Each medication shows its state. A ceiling shows as a bar with how much is left. A cycle medication is labelled as one. A paused one recedes rather than disappearing.
  • The MedlinePlus line earns its place, and says plainly whether the sentence came from MedlinePlus or was written by the app.

What changed and why

  • Two numbers first — doses taken and doses missed — because those are what a caregiver is asked at an appointment.
  • Charts get an axis. A trend line with no days under it is a decoration.
  • The temperature chart carries the line that matters, 100.4 °F, with a sentence saying what it is. A number is only useful next to the threshold it is being judged against.
  • Caught while drawing it: the first version put the 100.2 °F peak above the 100.4 line. The picture said "call the team" while the caption said the opposite — and the picture is what gets read at 2am.
